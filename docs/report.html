Analysis & inference strategies (W10)

What we did: we used the packages dplyr, tidyr, and ggplot2. Before the analyses, the dataset was cleaned by replacing the missing value codes (-98 and -99) with NA values. Only the variables relevant to the research questions were selected.

For the descriptive analysis, summary statistics and line plots were used to examine how maternity leave policies changed over time and differed across countries. These visualizations helped identify trends and country-specific developments.

For the explanatory analysis, the relationship between maternity leave duration and financial support was examined using correlation analysis and scatter plots. This allowed us to assess whether longer maternity leave is associated with higher levels of financial compensation.

To compare mandatory and voluntary maternity leave, a multiple linear regression model was estimated with leave duration as the dependent variable and year, leave type (mandatory vs. voluntary), and country as explanatory variables. An interaction term between year and leave type was included to test whether the development of the two leave types differed over time. Statistical significance was assessed using the regression coefficients and their associated p-values, while the coefficient of determination (R²) was used to evaluate the explanatory power of the model.

What worked well: The EDA was quite interesting and an important step beofre getting to fit the models, it helped understand the data better and what is trying to tell.

What was hard: accepting non-significant outcomes, deleting the predictive question due to our feedback

Key analytical decisions

Describe 2–3 decisions that had a meaningful effect on your results or workflow.

Decision 1: Flat rate vs. replacement rate

What were we deciding between? There are three different ways the finacial aid for parental leave is decided on. Depending on what measure you use, the meaning changes significantly.

How did we reason through it? if we relied on replacement rate, the aid would depend on how much the parent earned before. we would also have to look up how big the replacement rate would be, which would drastically change

In hindsight, would we decide differently?

Decision 2: Amount of observed countries

What were we deciding between? We were wondering if we should use all provided countries, a certain region or only a few selected.

How did we reason through it? We discussed alot about the statistical meaning being manipulated by too many NAs in certain countries, as well as having too chaotic plots, if all countries are being used. But scientifical completeness was more relevant for us than other arguments.

In hindsight, would we decide differently? In between we already designed plots for only 4 countries but eventually changed using all observed ones. Therefore, we wouldn’t decide differently.

Decision 3: parental vs. maternal leave

What were we deciding between? How we should differ or choose between variables considering maternal or parental leaves.

How did we reason through it? Again, we wondered how scientifical correct it was, leaving one out, but then argued with transparent communication. Also, it would’ve taken too much capacity for the course always considering both.

In hindsight, would we decide differently? No, only if we had more time.
